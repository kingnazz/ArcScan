ArcScan 1.8.2

What's new in ArcScan 1.8.2

ArcScan can now recognise more devices using mDNS and SSDP, while showing where each detected name and type came from. Printers, televisions, routers, cameras and media players already announce what they are on the local network. ArcScan asks, and tells you what it heard, along with how much of it to believe.

Better names

Devices that have a name now show it.

A printer that publishes Acme LaserFast 400 on the network used to appear as 192.168.1.31. Now it appears as what it calls itself, and the Inventory says which protocol that came from.

  • Names devices publish over mDNS
  • Names devices publish over SSDP
  • Manufacturer and model
  • Advertised services
  • Searchable
  • In every export

A name you typed always wins. That rule comes before every other one. ArcScan can name a device that had no name; it will never rename one you named. Where both exist, the drawer shows yours as the name and the detected one underneath, so nothing you chose is quietly replaced.

Names that describe a category rather than a device, such as printer, android or UPnP Device, are set aside, because two of them on one network would read as the same thing. A device calling itself printer keeps its hostname; one calling itself Front Office Printer does not.

Device types

What it is, and how sure that is.

ArcScan will call a device a printer, a router, a television, a camera, a NAS and ten other things, but only when something it saw actually supports that, and it always says how strong the support was.

Every type carries one of four confidence words:

  • High confidence: the device declared it, and something agrees
  • Medium confidence: one declaration, uncorroborated
  • Low confidence: inferred from a port or a manufacturer
  • Unknown: preferred over a guess

Four words rather than a percentage, because there is no sense in which a printing service is 0.7 of a printer, and a number invites arithmetic that is not justified. The confidence sits beside the type everywhere it appears, including in exports.

The device panel shows what the answer rests on, not just the answer. Where the evidence supports two readings, both are shown, because a storage device that also serves media is not a contradiction worth hiding. A wrong type is worse than no type, so ArcScan says Unknown rather than guessing.

Local only

It asks your network, and nothing else.

Discovery is the kind of feature that could quietly start talking to the internet. This one does not, and the rules are worth stating plainly.

Your network, or nothing

Discovery runs only when what you are scanning is inside a network this computer is actually attached to. Remote subnets, routed targets and public addresses never receive a single query. The queries are sent with a time-to-live of one, so they cannot leave the local link even if a router would have carried them.

No credentials, ever

ArcScan sends no username, no password, no community string and no key. It has none and asks for none. Everything it learns comes from what devices announce to anyone who listens.

No cloud service

Nothing about your network, your devices or your inventory is sent anywhere. There is no lookup service, no fingerprint database and no telemetry. Discovery talks to your local link and to nothing else.

Read-only, and bounded

ArcScan asks questions. It configures nothing, changes nothing and logs into nothing. Every listening window, every count and every document size has a hard limit, so a busy or misbehaving network costs the same few seconds as a quiet one.

Device pages are checked before they are read

When a device offers a description page, ArcScan reads it for the manufacturer and model, but only after checking that the address really is on the network being scanned. Public addresses, loopback, other private subnets, redirects and addresses carrying credentials are all refused, and the address that was checked is the address that gets used.

Everything a device says is treated as text

Names, models and descriptions come from unauthenticated devices, so ArcScan treats them as hostile input: bounded in length, stripped of control characters, and shown as text rather than interpreted. Nothing a device sends can act on your machine.

You can switch all of it off in Settings, and switch off the description-page reading on its own. The privacy page lists every request ArcScan makes.

Upgrading

Install it over 1.8 and carry on.

Install ArcScan 1.8.2 over 1.8, 1.7 or 1.6 without losing scans, device names, notes, statuses, network names or history.

Your inventory is kept

Every scan, device, name, note, status, network and date carries straight over. The database gains two tables for what devices announce, and nothing already in it is rewritten. No database change is needed beyond that, and coming from 1.7 or 1.6 the usual 1.8 upgrade runs once, in place.

Your settings are kept

Theme, density, columns, profiles, ports and limits are all preserved. Local discovery is switched on for new installs and for upgrades, and can be switched off in Settings at any time, along with the reading of device description pages on its own.

Nothing is renamed

A device you named keeps your name for it. A device you never named may gain a name it announces, shown alongside the address it used to show. Nothing you wrote is overwritten, and no device is merged with another because two of them announce the same thing.

Unchanged

Everything 1.8 does, it still does.

Discovery adds to what ArcScan knows about a device. It changes nothing about how devices are recognised, or what a scan means.

  • The same four views
  • The same scanning behaviour
  • Devices still matched by MAC, then hostname, then address
  • A detected name never merges two devices
  • The same present, missing and unknown rules
  • Partial scans still never mark a device missing
  • The same networks and network names
  • The same history and comparisons
  • The same CSV, JSON and XML exports
  • No account, no cloud, no subscription
  • Scans and inventory stay on your computer
  • IPv4 only
  • Installers are not code signed

An IPv6 address a device announces is shown as extra information, with a note saying so. ArcScan scans IPv4 only, and showing an address is not a claim that anything was scanned at it. Because the installers carry no paid publisher certificate, Windows and macOS both warn the first time you open one. Coming from an earlier version?
